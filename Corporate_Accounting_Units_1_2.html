Share Capital, Forfeiture & Buy-Back

From issuance mechanics to buy-backs, underwriting and bonus shares.

🍕 The Pizza Analogy — Share Capital at a Glance

A company is a massive pizza. Share Capital = total pizza. Each share = one slice. Shareholders are co-owners.
• Reserve Capital — Emergency slice locked in the fridge; only available if the company winds up (Sec 65).
• Capital Reserve — A windfall profit piggy-bank from capital profits (e.g., profit on re-issue of forfeited shares).

3. Securities Premium — Sec 52 (5 Uses Only)

★ Exam Trap: Securities Premium is NOT freely distributable. It can ONLY be used for these 5 specific purposes. Any other use is illegal.
5 Permitted Uses
  1. Issue fully paid Bonus Shares
  2. Write off Preliminary Expenses
  3. Write off discount or expenses on issue of shares/debentures
  4. Provide for premium payable on redemption of preference shares/debentures
  5. Fund buy-back of own shares (Sec 68)
Special Types of Share Issue
  • Right Shares (Sec 62): New shares must first be offered to existing shareholders proportionally.
  • ESOP: Right to buy shares at a future date at a pre-determined price (for employees/directors).
  • Sweat Equity (Sec 54): Issued at discount or for non-cash consideration (know-how, IP).
  • Bonus Shares: Free shares from accumulated reserves — no cash received.

6. Buy-Back of Shares (Sec 68–70)

🔄 The "Destroying Pizza Slices" Analogy

Buy-back = the company spends its own savings to repurchase slices from shareholders and permanently destroys those slices. Fewer slices exist → each remaining slice becomes more valuable (EPS improves). But there's a legal cap so the company doesn't bankrupt itself buying back shares.

Sources for Buy-Back
  • Free Reserves
  • Securities Premium Account
  • Proceeds of any other specified securities (NOT from proceeds of same equity issue)
Key Conditions (Sec 68)
  • Authorised by Articles of Association
  • Max 25% of paid-up equity capital + free reserves (per financial year)
  • Post buy-back Debt:Equity ratio must be ≤ 2:1
  • Shares must be fully paid-up
  • Bought-back shares destroyed within 7 days
Sec 69 — Mandatory CRR Transfer: When buy-back is from Free Reserves or Securities Premium, transfer an amount equal to the nominal (face) value of shares bought back to Capital Redemption Reserve (CRR).

7. Underwriting

🛡 The IPO Insurance Analogy

Underwriting = buying insurance for a share issue. If the public under-subscribes, underwriters are contractually bound to buy the unsold shares. For taking this risk they earn a commission.
Max Commission: 5% on Shares | 2.5% on Debentures.

Net Liability of Each Underwriter
Gross Liability
− Marked Applications
− Share of Unmarked Applications (in Gross ratio)
− Surplus of any co-underwriter (distributed in Gross ratio)
= Net Liability

Accounting for Debentures

🏢 The Crowd-Loan Analogy

Shares make people owners; debentures make them lenders. Instead of going to one bank, the company borrows small amounts from thousands of people and gives each one a certificate (debenture). Fixed interest must be paid regardless of profit/loss — it is a charge against profit, not an appropriation like dividend.

3. DRR & DRI

💷 The Piggy Bank + Investment Analogy

You owe Rs. 50 Lakhs in 5 years. Paying it all at once = cash-flow disaster. So every year you lock a slice of profit into a DRR piggy bank, then invest that money outside the business (DRI investments) so it earns interest. In year 5 you sell the investments, smash the piggy bank, and pay off the debt smoothly. Whatever is left in DRF transfers to General Reserve.

Type of CompanyDRR Required
All India Financial Institutions / Banking CompaniesNIL
NBFCs & HFCs — Publicly Issued Debentures25%
NBFCs & HFCs — Privately Placed DebenturesNIL
Other Companies (Listed or Unlisted)25%
DRI Investment Requirement
Invest ≥ 15% of debentures maturing in the year ending 31st March next year
Deadline: 30th April each year. Invest in Govt bonds, FDs or specified securities.
Sinking Fund Method — Complete Journal Entry Set
Last Year Rule: NO new investment in last year. Sell investments → redeem debentures → transfer DRF balance to General Reserve.

Accounting Standards — AS 10 & AS 2

AS 10: Property, Plant and Equipment (PPE)

Definition: Tangible items held for production, supply of goods/services, rental, or administrative purposes, expected to be used for more than one accounting period (> 12 months).
🏗 The "Ready to Produce the First Widget" Rule

Capitalize only costs needed to get the asset to its location and into its intended working condition. Once it's running, maintenance, training, and admin are all expensed. Simple rule: Before the first widget = capitalize. After = expense.

✓ Costs Included (Capitalized)✗ Costs Excluded (Expensed to P&L)

Less: Trade discounts and rebatesAdvertising and promotional activities
Site preparation, delivery & handling chargesInitial operating losses before commercial production
Installation and assembly costsStaff training costs
Professional / consultants' fees (architects, engineers)General & administrative overheads
Initial estimate of PV of dismantling/restoration costsRelocation / reorganisation costs
Borrowing costs (if qualifying asset under AS 16)Interest on deferred credit to supplier

Subsequent Measurement Models
  • Cost Model: Cost − Accumulated Depreciation − Impairment Losses
  • Revaluation Model: Fair Value at revaluation date − subsequent depreciation − impairment. Entire class must be revalued. Must be done with sufficient regularity.
Revaluation Accounting
  • Value Increases: Cr. Revaluation Surplus (Reserve) — NOT P&L
  • Value Decreases: Dr. P&L (first reduce existing surplus for that asset, then P&L)
  • On disposal: remaining revaluation surplus transferred to Retained Earnings directly

Depreciation Rules under AS 10

  • Component Approach: Significant parts depreciated separately (e.g., aircraft engine vs. fuselage).
  • Depreciation starts when asset is available for use (intended location & condition).
  • Depreciation continues even when idle (unless fully depreciated).
  • Land = unlimited life → NOT depreciated. Buildings ARE depreciated separately.
  • Residual value, useful life, and method reviewed at every year-end.

AS 2: Valuation of Inventory

📦 The Pessimist Accountant Analogy

Accountants follow the Prudence concept: if stock bought at Rs. 100 can only sell for Rs. 80 today, record the Rs. 20 loss immediately. But if it can sell for Rs. 150, wait until it's actually sold. Hence: value inventory at whichever is lower — Cost or NRV.

Core Principle (AS 2)
Inventory Value = LOWER of Cost and Net Realisable Value (NRV)
What is NRV?

Estimated selling price in ordinary course of business
Less: Estimated costs of completion
Less: Estimated costs necessary to make the sale

Raw Material Exception: If finished goods will sell at or above cost, raw materials are NOT written down even if their price fell.

Cost Formulas Permitted
  • FIFO: Oldest stock sold first. Closing stock reflects latest (higher) prices.
  • Weighted Average: Smoothed price; good for homogeneous goods.
  • Specific Identification: Unique high-value items (cars, machinery).

LIFO is NOT permitted under AS 2.

✓ Costs Included in Inventory✗ Costs Excluded (Expensed)
Purchase price + import duties + taxesAbnormal waste (material, labour, overhead)
Direct labour & other direct costsStorage costs (unless part of production process)
Fixed & variable production overheads (absorbed)Administrative overheads unrelated to production
Costs to bring to present location & conditionSelling & distribution costs

Smoothens price fluctuations; better for frequently traded homogeneous goods.

Key Numbers & Limits

Buy-Back (Sec 68–70)

  • Max: 25% of paid-up equity + free reserves (per year)
  • Post buy-back Debt:Equity ≤ 2:1
  • CRR transfer = Nominal value of cancelled shares
  • Destroy shares within 7 days

Securities Premium (Sec 52)

  • Bonus Shares
  • Preliminary Expenses write-off
  • Discount/Expenses on Issue write-off
  • Redemption Premium on Pref Shares / Deb
  • Buy-Back (Sec 68)

DRR & DRI Rules

  • Banks & AIFIs: NIL
  • NBFCs (public issue): 25%
  • Others: 25% of outstanding debentures
  • DRI: ≥ 15% of maturing debentures by 30 April

Underwriting Commission Limits

  • Shares: Max 5%
  • Debentures: Max 2.5%
  • Net Liability = Gross − Marked − Unmarked share − Surplus

Bonus Shares — Source Order

  • 1. Capital Redemption Reserve (CRR)
  • 2. Securities Premium A/c
  • 3. General Reserve
  • 4. P&L Account
  • ❌ NOT: Revaluation Reserve

AS 2 — Inventory Valuation

  • Value = Lower of Cost and NRV
  • Allowed: FIFO, Weighted Average, Specific ID
  • NOT allowed: LIFO
  • NRV = Selling Price − Completion Costs − Selling Costs

AS 10 — Capitalize vs Expense

  • ✓ Site prep, delivery, installation, professional fees
  • ✓ PV of dismantling costs
  • ✗ Training, admin, advertising
  • ✗ Pre-commercial operating losses
  • ✗ Interest (unless qualifying asset — AS 16)

Debenture Issue — Loss Recognition

  • Issued at Discount → Dr. Discount on Issue
  • Redeemable at Premium → Dr. Loss on Issue + Cr. Premium on Redemption (at issue date)
  • Both: Total loss = Discount + Redemption Premium

Forfeiture — Capital Reserve Formula

  • Forfeited A/c balance (on re-issued shares)
  • Less: Discount allowed on re-issue
  • = Transfer to Capital Reserve

Preference Shares Redemption (Sec 55)

  • Must be fully paid-up before redemption
  • Fund from: Profits + Fresh Issue of Shares
  • If from profits → transfer nominal value to CRR
  • Premium on redemption → from Securities Premium or P&L

Classic Exam Confusions

  • Capital Reserve ≠ Reserve Capital
  • Interest on Deb = Charge; Dividend = Appropriation
  • DRF surplus after redemption → General Reserve
  • Revaluation Surplus → NOT distributable as dividend
  • Last year of DRF → NO new investment

AS 10 — Depreciation Quick Rules

  • Land: NOT depreciated (unlimited life)
  • Component approach: significant parts depreciated separately
  • Starts when asset is available for use
  • Continues even when idle
  • Review residual value, life & method every year-end

Journal Entry Mnemonics

Forfeiture — Remember "CCCA"
  • Called-up value → Dr. Share Capital A/c
  • Credited amount (unpaid premium) → Dr. Securities Premium
  • Calls in Arrears → always on Credit side
  • Amount paid → Cr. Share Forfeited A/c
Debenture Issue — Remember "BDLP"
  • Bank → Dr. (cash received)
  • Discount on Issue → Dr. (immediate loss)
  • Loss on Issue → Dr. (for future redemption premium)
  • Premium on Redemption → Cr. (liability created)
